Potentiometric titrations remain a cornerstone of analytical
chemistry but require titrant and solvent volumes in excess due
to full-curve exploration to locate the equivalence point (EQP),
contributing in essence to unnecessary waste and contradicting
green chemistry principles. Here, we introduce a sustainable
two-step analytical strategy that systematically reduces solvent
consumption while maintaining high accuracy in EQP determina-
tion. An initial conventional titration identifies the pre-equivalence
linear region via Gran and Schwartz linearization methods. The
central point of this segment serves as the truncation limit for
subsequent measurements using smaller fixed increments, enabling
precise EQP extrapolation from limited data. Applied to the
aqueous titration of test case TRIS with HCl, this approach yields
EQP values with excellent repeatability (standard error comparable
to or better than traditional full-titration methods across n=6
replicates each). Solvent consumption is reduced by 20-27% per
analysis (e.g., 1 mL saved relative to reaching the EQP or 1.5 mL
vs. full endpoint), directly supporting Principles 1 and 5 of green
chemistry. Implemented in the open-source Python tool GranTED
(available at https://github.com/sgiani95/GranTED), this
protocol offers a practical, low-waste alternative for routine and
educational potentiometric analyses.
Introduction
Titration is a quantitative chemical analysis method used to de-
termine the concentration of a substance (analyte) in solution.
A reagent of known concentration (titrant) is gradually added to
the analyte until the reaction is complete. The endpoint is de-
tected using a color indicator or instrumental measurements (pH,

from low accuracy in systems with small potential breaks, such
as weak electrolytes or asymmetric curves. To address this, Gran
introduced differential plotting of ∆V /∆E or ∆V /∆pH versus V
in 1950, ? yielding near-linear segments intersecting at the EQP.
In 1952, Gran refined this with antilogarithmic transformations
(e.g., (V ) × 10−pH versus V ), ? producing straight lines for easier
extrapolation. Schwartz further advanced Gran methodology in
1980’s by incorporating corrections for significant [H+] or [OH−]
perturbing effects, enabling analysis of challenging cases. ?
Despite these methodological improvements, potentiometric
titrations remain resource-intensive, requiring full-curve acqui-
sitions that consume excess solvents and reagents, generating
unnecessary waste contrary to green chemistry principles (e.g.,
waste prevention and safer solvents). ? In this work, we present
a sustainable analytical strategy that minimizes solvent use while
leveraging Gran (1952) and Schwartz linearizations for precise
EQP determination.
The protocol involves an initial conventional titration, un-
til post-EQP, to identify the pre-EQP linear region. The cen-
tral point of this segment then defines the truncation limit
for subsequent titrations, enabling EQP extrapolation from re-
duced data, obtained using reduced titrant. Applied to TRIS-
HCl titrations (n=6 replicates per condition), this yields EQPs
with repeatability comparable to traditional full titrations and
with 20–27% solvent reduction per analysis (e.g., 1 mL saved
versus EQP or 1.5 mL vs. full titration endpoint). This
approach supports Green Chemistry Principles 1 and 5, ? of-
fering a greener tool for routine and educational analyses.

Experimental Section
Materials and Instrumentation
Tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane (TRIS, 99.97%, Merck
Sigma-Aldrich) was titrated with hydrochloric acid (HCl, 0.1 M).
Solutions were prepared in deionized water (18.2 MΩ·cm). Po-
tentiometric measurements used a Mettler Toledo T5 autoburette
with a glass electrode. All experiments were conducted at room
temperature.
Protocols
Initial dynamic titration: 5 mL TRIS (0.1 M), diluted with 60
mL deionized water, with variable HCl increments (0.5–1 mL pre-
buffer, 0.05 mL near EQP) until post-EQP. Data processed for in-
flection point and with Schwartz-modified Gran plots to identify
pre-EQP linear region (R2 > 0.999), central point and EQP. Vali-
dation: n=6 replicates with fixed 0.05 mL increments up to trun-
cated endpoint (pre-EQP). EQP extrapolated from linear fits; un-
certainties from regression.
Data Analysis
Implemented in Python-based GranTED (v1.0, MIT license,
https://github.com/sgiani95/GranTED). Statistics: mean ±
SD (n=6); solvent savings calculated as volume differences vs.
EQP and titration endpoint. Full equations, raw data, and algo-
rithm pseudocode in Electronic Supporting Information (ESI).
Results and Discussion
Method Development: Dynamic Titration for Linear Range
Identification
The sustainable analytical strategy begins with a single conven-
tional dynamic titration to establish the baseline pH − V curve
and identify the pre-equivalence linear region necessary for sub-
sequent truncations. For the titration of 5 mL of TRIS (0.1 M),
diluted with 60 mL deionized water, with HCl (0.1 M), the ini-
tial dynamic run (Figure 1a) yields a sigmoidal curve typical of
weak base-strong acid systems (pKa(TRIS) ≈ 8.1). Applying the
Schwartz-modified Gran transformation ((V + k) × 10−pH vs. V ,
Figure 1b) linearizes the pre-EQP segment. The linear region
(shaded in Figure 1b) spans approximately 3.5–4.5 mL, with its
central point (4 mL) determined via automated fitting (R2 >
0.9995). This point, safely before the EQP to avoid overshoot,
serves as the truncation limit for validation measurements, reduc-
ing titrant waste consumption while enabling extrapolation-based
EQP determination akin to Gran’s functions intersections. ?
EQP Determination via Extrapolation
Determination from the pre-EQP linear fit provides the EQP with-
out requiring full post-equivalence data. In the initial, dynamic
run, the Schwartz plot (Figure 1b) intersects the V-axis at 5 mL
(theoretical EQP), with uncertainty propagated from linear re-
gression (± 0.010 mL). This aligns with Gran’s 1952 theory for
transforming parabolic branches into straight lines, ? enhanced
by Schwartz’s adjustments to account for [H+] dominance in the
buffer region. ? For TRIS-HCl, the method yields precise EQPs
even in noisy data, as the linear slope reflects system strength
(steeper for stronger interactions). The central truncation point
ensures the fit uses only reliable pre-EQP data.
Method Validation: High-Resolution Fixed-Increment Titra-
tion
Subsequent validations confirm the strategy’s accuracy using
fixed smaller increments (e.g., 0.05 mL) up to the truncation
point (Figure 1c). The corresponding Schwartz plot (Figure 1d)
mirrors the dynamic linearization, extrapolating to an EQP of 5
mL (± 0.005 mL), matching the reference value within experi-
mental error. Across n=6 replicates per method, repeatability is
excellent: standard deviation for truncated EQPs (0.002 mL) is
even lower to conventional full titrations (0.005 mL; Table 1).
Standard errors remain low (0.005%), demonstrating robustness
against volume variability. This validation echoes Schwartz’s sim-
ulations for weak acids, where modified plots straighten curva-
ture, ? extending it however to a practical truncation protocol for
real-world efficiency.
Green Chemistry Metrics and Sustainability Assessment
The truncation yields systematic solvent/titrant savings, quanti-
fied as 1 mL vs. the EQP and 1.5 mL vs. the full endpoint per
the illustrated analysis (Figure 2; 20% and 27% reductions, re-
spectively). For routine labs performing multiple replicates, this
accumulates to significant waste minimization, directly embody-
ing Green Chemistry Principle 1 (prevention) by avoiding un-
necessary full runs and Principle 5 (safer/less solvents) through
reduced HCl exposure. ? Compared to traditional methods re-
quiring titrant-in-excess curves, ? ? ? our approach decreases re-
source use without compromising accuracy and precision (Table
1). Implemented in the open-source Python tool GranTED, this
facilitates method development, method validation and validated
method automated linear detection and metrics calculation, pro-
moting adoption in educational and monitoring contexts where
sustainability is paramount.
Conclusions
In conclusion, we have developed a green analytical strategy for
potentiometric EQP determination that leverages Gran–Schwartz
linearizations to enable truncation at the pre-EQP linear region’s
central point, systematically reducing solvent/titrant consump-
tion by ≈ 20% savings. Applied to TRIS-HCl titrations, it delivers
EQPs with repeatability (SD ± 0.002 mL, n=6) comparable to
conventional methods, aligning with green chemistry Principles
1, and 5 through waste prevention and resource efficiency. This
protocol, implemented in open-source GranTED software, offers
a sustainable alternative for routine lab and educational appli-
cations, with potential extensions to complex mixtures, environ-
mental samples, as well as automated high-throughput systems.
