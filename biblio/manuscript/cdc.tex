\section*{Introduction}

Titration is a quantitative chemical analysis method used to determine the concentration of a substance (analyte) in solution. A reagent of known concentration (titrant) is gradually added to the analyte until the reaction is complete. The endpoint is detected using a color indicator or instrumental measurements (pH, potential E).
Traditional methods rely on identifying the inflection point in E or pH versus volume ($V$) curves, but these often suffer from low accuracy in systems with small potential breaks, such as weak electrolytes or asymmetric curves. To address this, Gran introduced differential plotting of $\Delta V/\Delta E$ or $\Delta V/\Delta pH$ versus $V$ in 1950,\cite{gran1950determination} yielding near-linear segments intersecting at the EQP. In 1952, Gran refined this with antilogarithmic transformations (e.g., $(V) \times 10^{-pH}$ versus $V$),\cite{gran1952determination} producing straight lines for easier extrapolation. Schwartz further advanced Gran methodology in 1980's by incorporating corrections for significant \ce{[H+]} or \ce{[OH-]} perturbing effects, enabling analysis of challenging cases.\cite{schwartz1987advances}

Despite these methodological improvements, potentiometric titrations remain resource-intensive, requiring full-curve acquisitions that consume excess solvents and reagents, generating unnecessary waste contrary to green chemistry principles (e.g., waste prevention and safer solvents).\cite{anastas1998green} In this work, we present a sustainable analytical strategy that minimizes solvent use while leveraging Gran (1952) and Schwartz linearizations for precise EQP determination.

The protocol involves an initial conventional titration, until post-EQP, to identify the pre-EQP linear region. The central point of this segment then defines the truncation limit for subsequent titrations, enabling EQP extrapolation from reduced data, obtained using reduced titrant. Applied to TRIS-\ce{HCl} titrations (n=6 replicates per condition), this yields EQPs with repeatability comparable to traditional full titrations and with 20–27\% solvent reduction per analysis (e.g., 1 mL saved versus EQP or 1.5 mL vs. full titration endpoint). This approach supports Green Chemistry Principles 1 and 5,\cite{anastas1998green} offering a greener tool for routine and educational analyses. Open-source implementation in Python (\textsf{GranTED}, available at \href{https://github.com/sgiani95/GranTED}{\texttt{https://github.com/sgiani95/GranTED}}) facilitates its adoption.

\section*{Experimental Section}

\subsection*{Materials and Instrumentation}

Tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane (TRIS, 99.97\%, Merck Sigma-Aldrich) was titrated with hydrochloric acid (\ce{HCl}, 0.1 M, Titripac Merck Millipore). Solutions were prepared in deionized water (18.2 M$\Omega$·cm). Potentiometric measurements have been performed by means of a Mettler-Toledo T5 automated titrator equipped with a combined pH glass electrode. All experiments were conducted at room temperature.

\subsection*{Protocols}

Initial dynamic titration: 5 mL TRIS (0.1 M), diluted with 60 mL deionized water, with variable \ce{HCl} increments (0.5–1 mL pre-buffer, 0.05 mL near EQP) until post-EQP. Data processed for inflection point and with Schwartz-modified Gran plots to identify pre-EQP linear region ($R^2$ > 0.999), central point and EQP. Validation: n=6 replicates with fixed 0.05 mL increments up to truncated endpoint (pre-EQP). EQP extrapolated from linear fits; uncertainties from regression.

\subsection*{Data Analysis}

Implemented in Python-based \textsf{GranTED} (v1.0, MIT license, \href{https://github.com/sgiani95/GranTED}{\texttt{https://github.com/sgiani95/GranTED}}). Statistics: mean ± SD (n=6); solvent savings calculated as volume differences vs. EQP and titration endpoint. Full equations, raw data, and algorithm pseudocode in Electronic Supporting Information (ESI).

\section*{Results and Discussion}

\subsection*{Method Development: Dynamic Titration for Linear Range Identification}

The sustainable analytical strategy begins with a single conventional dynamic titration to establish the baseline $pH-V$ curve and identify the pre-equivalence linear region necessary for subsequent truncations. For the titration of 5 mL of TRIS (0.1 M), diluted with 60 mL deionized water, with \ce{HCl} (0.1 M), the initial dynamic run (Figure 1a) yields a sigmoidal curve typical of weak base-strong acid systems ($pK_a$(TRIS) $\approx$ 8.1). Applying the Schwartz-modified Gran transformation ($(V+k) \times 10^{-pH}$ vs. $V$, Figure 1b) linearizes the pre-EQP segment. The linear region (shaded in Figure 1b) spans approximately 3.5–4.5 mL, with its central point (4 mL) determined via automated fitting ($R^2$ > 0.9995). This point, safely before the EQP to avoid overshoot, serves as the truncation limit for validation measurements, reducing titrant waste consumption while enabling extrapolation-based EQP determination akin to Gran's functions intersections.\cite{gran1950determination}

\subsection*{EQP Determination via Extrapolation}

Determination from the pre-EQP linear fit provides the EQP without requiring full post-equivalence data. In the initial, dynamic run, the Schwartz plot (Figure 1b) intersects the V-axis at 5 mL (theoretical EQP), with uncertainty propagated from linear regression (± 0.010 mL). This aligns with Gran's 1952 theory for transforming parabolic branches into straight lines,\cite{gran1952determination} enhanced by Schwartz's adjustments to account for \ce[H+] dominance in the buffer region.\cite{schwartz1987advances} For TRIS-\ce{HCl}, the method yields precise EQPs even in noisy data, as the linear slope reflects system strength (steeper for stronger interactions). The central truncation point ensures the fit uses only reliable pre-EQP data.

\subsection*{Method Validation: High-Resolution Fixed-Increment Titration}

Subsequent validations confirm the strategy's accuracy using fixed smaller increments (e.g., 0.05 mL) up to the truncation point (Figure 1c). The corresponding Schwartz plot (Figure 1d) mirrors the dynamic linearization, extrapolating to an EQP of 5 mL (± 0.005 mL), matching the reference value within experimental error. Across n=6 replicates per method, repeatability is excellent: standard deviation for truncated EQPs (0.002 mL) is even lower to conventional full titrations (0.005 mL; Table 1). Standard errors remain low (0.005\%), demonstrating robustness against volume variability. This validation echoes Schwartz's simulations for weak acids, where modified plots straighten curvature,\cite{schwartz1987advances} extending it however to a practical truncation protocol for real-world efficiency.

\subsection*{Green Chemistry Metrics and Sustainability Assessment}

The truncation yields systematic titrant savings, quantified as 1 mL vs. the EQP and 1.5 mL vs. the full endpoint per the illustrated analysis (Figure 2; 20\% and 27\% reductions, respectively). For routine labs performing multiple replicates, this accumulates to significant waste minimization, directly embodying Green Chemistry Principle 1 (prevention) by avoiding unnecessary full runs and Principle 5 (safer/less solvents) through reduced \ce{HCl} exposure.\cite{anastas1998green} Compared to traditional methods requiring titrant-in-excess curves,\cite{gran1950determination,gran1952determination,schwartz1987advances} our approach decreases resource use without compromising accuracy and precision (Table 1). Implemented in the open-source Python tool \textsf{GranTED}, this facilitates method development, method validation and validated method automated linear detection and metrics calculation, promoting adoption in educational and monitoring contexts where sustainability is paramount.

\subsection*{Conclusions}

In conclusion, we have developed a green analytical strategy for potentiometric EQP determination that leverages Gran–Schwartz linearizations to enable truncation at the pre-EQP linear region's central point, systematically reducing solvent/titrant consumption by $\approx$ 20\% savings. Applied to TRIS-\ce{HCl} titrations, it delivers EQPs with repeatability (SD ± 0.002 mL, n=6) comparable to conventional methods, aligning with green chemistry Principles 1, and 5 through waste prevention and resource efficiency. This protocol, implemented in open-source \textsf{GranTED} software, offers a sustainable alternative for routine lab and educational applications, with potential extensions to complex mixtures, environmental samples, as well as automated high-throughput systems.
